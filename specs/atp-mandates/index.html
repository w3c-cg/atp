This proposal defines a semantic model for portable, cryptographically verifiable, versioned and revocable delegated authority for autonomous and semi-autonomous software agents. It separates identity, authority, runtime policy, enforcement and evidence. It does not select a normative wire format or require any particular vendor, identity method or agent transport.

Individual contributor proposal, 25 November 2026. This is not an adopted Community Group Report, recorded consensus, W3C Recommendation or conformance certification. All requirements are proposed for review. The existing four ATP drafts do not define a general-purpose mandate profile. Experimental ATP Core fixtures inform this proposal but do not establish complete implementation or independent interoperability.

Scope and terminology

A mandate expresses bounded authority from an authorized issuer to a subject. The accountable principal is the originating human or organization; the root grant issuer is the originating accountable grant authority. An issuer acting for another principal needs verifiable authority to do so. Technical accountability does not determine legal liability.

A delegator issues a narrower child grant; a verifier validates the grant and holder proof; a policy decision point evaluates local policy; a policy enforcement point controls the protected resource. Evidence verifiers evaluate claims about decisions and, separately, execution.

Identity and trust evidence do not themselves grant authority. A valid mandate is necessary where required, but does not override local policy.

Semantic data model

Proposed fields: id, schemaVersion, grantVersion, issuer, principal, subject, audience, rootGrant, parentGrant, actions, resources, constraints, issuedAt, notBefore, expiresAt, delegation, statusRef and proof. References to parent and root grants must bind the relevant version and authenticated content. A root grant has no parent; the root reference for a root may be implicit. This is a semantic model, not a wire format.

Proposed requirements

Capitalized requirement words are used as described in BCP 14 [[RFC2119]] [[RFC8174]]. These requirements are provisional and do not establish a complete conformance profile.

  1. MND-001: A mandate MUST identify its issuer and authorized subject.
  2. MND-002: A verifier MUST establish issuer authority to issue the grant.
  3. MND-003: Authority MUST be explicitly bounded by actions, resources and constraints.
  4. MND-004: Authenticity and integrity MUST be cryptographically verifiable.
  5. MND-005: The requester MUST demonstrate possession of an authorized key or equivalent holder binding.
  6. MND-006: Requests MUST be bound to the resource, audience and operation.
  7. MND-007: Delegation MUST NOT exceed parent authority.
  8. MND-008: Redelegation MUST NOT occur absent explicit permission.
  9. MND-009: Verifiers MUST reject expired, revoked, paused or otherwise invalid authority under the applicable status profile.
  10. MND-010: Protected execution MUST fail closed if required current authority cannot be established.
  11. MND-011: Replay protections MUST prevent unauthorized reuse of action-bound requests.
  12. MND-012: A valid mandate MUST NOT override more restrictive local policy.
  13. MND-013: Protected actions MUST cross an enforcement boundary the requesting agent cannot override within the declared threat model.
  14. MND-014: Decision evidence MUST identify the evaluated grant version and policy context.
  15. MND-015: Decision evidence MUST NOT be represented as successful execution without supporting execution evidence.
  16. MND-016: Supported protocol boundaries MUST preserve relevant constraints and authority lineage.
  17. MND-017: Verifiers MUST reject unsupported critical semantics.
  18. MND-018: Implementations SHOULD minimize unnecessary disclosure of principal and delegation information.

Delegation and lifecycle

Each child grant must narrow or preserve every applicable restriction, including action, resource, audience, validity period, purpose, depth and approval conditions. Unknown constraint comparisons fail closed. Validate the complete chain, issuer-to-parent-subject binding, and current ancestor status; reject cycles. Aggregate budgets require coordinated accounting, not only per-grant limits.

Proposed states: issued, active, paused, expired and revoked. Resume requires authenticated authorization and current validity. Expiry applies while paused. Revocation is terminal for the revoked grant; renewed authority requires a new authorized grant. Status changes and replacements must not permit rollback to invalid versions.

Runtime enforcement and revocation

Evaluate authenticated holder and issuer evidence, chain, action binding, freshness, constraints, replay and local policy before protected execution. A protected executor must validate the decision binding and required current status; an agent ignoring a refusal must not obtain access through the declared enforcement boundary.

Proposed baseline: fresh status read, no cached fallback for protected actions requiring current status; exact freshness and clock rules remain open. Distinguish pre-dispatch refusal from in-flight revocation and committed effects. Specify the consistency guarantee and commitment point. Revocation cannot undo an already committed irreversible action. Strong atomic revocation/execution guarantees are not established.

Security and privacy considerations

Threats include stolen credentials and keys, issuer impersonation, replay, confused deputy, resource or tenant substitution, authority escalation, stale status, verifier outage, concurrent budget consumption, direct bypass and forged outcome claims. Proof of key possession does not prove trustworthy workload identity. Trusted workload measurement may be an additional policy requirement; self-asserted measurement is insufficient. Minimize identifiers and sensitive action details; restrict access to evidence, preserve necessary bindings, and avoid cross-context correlation.

Interoperability and open decisions

Evaluate existing DID, VC, OAuth and authorization decision/enforcement mechanisms before selecting a format. Candidate transport mappings include MCP, A2A and ANP, without claiming tested interoperability. Open decisions: canonical encoding and signature suite, holder binding, root trust, delegation comparison, authenticated status, freshness and consistency, approval consumption, algorithm agility, privacy, refusal codes and evidence format. No post-quantum mandate profile is established.

Conformance and implementation evidence

The accompanying scenario catalog is non-normative and not executable wire vectors. No complete conformance claim is possible until profiles and harnesses are specified.
